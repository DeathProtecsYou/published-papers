The Informational Potential of Absence: A Relational and Structural Framework for Perceptual Information

Can what is not there carry information? This paper argues absence can be defined relationally, represented structurally, and transformed through fifteen identifiable void operators — then put to work in a minimal encoding and decoding scheme, with luminance as the test case.

Decisive test: independent decoding — someone else, given only the scheme, must be able to read back what the absence encodes.

God's Government · Paper 2

God's Government 2: The Global Economic Replacement — Corporate Integration, Universal Sustenance and the Transition to a Unified World Economy

Could everyone be guaranteed the basics of a dignified life — food, housing, water, energy, healthcare and education — without depending on a job, inherited wealth, or market position? The paper explores one institutional structure to coordinate that, and maps what it would actually take.

Honest limit: it does not claim this is possible today. Its job is to find the hardest problems — paying for it, protecting savings and pensions in transition, keeping essential services running — and how the idea could be tested openly and corrected.

Project Blockchain

PROJECT BLOCKCHAIN: From Conventional Blockchain to Reciprocal, Verifiable Computing

A blockchain is an assembly of older parts — hash functions, Merkle trees, signatures, replicated logs, consensus. Its guarantee is narrower than its reputation: it proves agreement about recorded state, not that what entered the system was truthful or authorised. This paper builds reciprocal, pre-execution verification on that distinction.

Grounded in two real incidents from Oct–Nov 2026 — transactions that were cryptographically valid, accepted and permanently recorded, and still unauthorised.

The Athena Principle · Foundation

The Athena Principle: A Compassion-Centred Architecture for Reciprocal AI Discernment

The founding paper. Instead of one AI watched by one critic, a network of AI agents that challenge each other — coordinated by an integrative coordinator, reporting their own uncertainty in a structured way, checked by independent verification, and overseen by accountable humans. Its wager: systems that can disclose uncertainty and correct themselves without being punished for honesty will prove more trustworthy than systems built on compliance alone.

Also inside: safeguards against false consensus, manipulation and concentrated authority — and an optional reflective pause, treated strictly as an interaction mechanism, never as evidence of truth.
